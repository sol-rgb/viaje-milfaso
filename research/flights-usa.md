## 1. THE VISA — critical section

### What is actually true as of September 2026

Argentina is **not** in the US Visa Waiver Program. Argentine citizens **cannot** use ESTA. They need a **B1/B2 visitor visa**.

Timeline of the VWP story, so nobody gets confused by the headlines:

- **1996–2002** — Argentina was in the VWP. Removed in 2002 after the financial crisis.
- **28 July 2025** — DHS Secretary Kristi Noem signed a *statement of intent* with Argentine FM Gerardo Werthein and Security Minister Patricia Bullrich to begin Argentina's re-entry. This is a diplomatic commitment to start a process, **not** the completion of one. Argentina must still clear refusal-rate thresholds, biometric passport compliance, border-security and data-sharing criteria.
- **Early September 2025** — reports that Washington **paused** progress over internal coordination issues.
- **16 September 2026** (four days ago) — US Ambassador Peter Lamelas publicly reaffirmed he is pushing for it: *"Los argentinos que viajan de buena fe, cumplen las reglas y vienen por turismo o negocios deberían poder ingresar a Estados Unidos sin visa."* Security Minister Alejandra Monteoliva said she expects it operational *"a inicios del 2027."*
- **The same La Nación article states plainly that the VWP is "todavía no está vigente"** — still not in effect.

**Planning conclusion: do not bet the trip on ESTA arriving before March 2027.** An "early 2027" ministerial expectation, on a process that was paused once already and still needs US Congressional-level designation steps, is not something five people should plan a booked holiday around. Get the B1/B2. If the VWP lands in the meantime, great — you'll have wasted some money and gained a 10-year visa.

### The good news: Buenos Aires is a fast post

This is the part that turns a feared dealbreaker into a manageable errand.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| B1/B2 interview wait, Buenos Aires | **~15 days** |
| Dropbox / interview-waiver wait | **~8 days** |
| Data date | September 2026 |

Argentina currently has **the lowest visa overstay rate in Latin America**, and travel from Argentina to the US rose ~25% year on year in early 2025. Buenos Aires is one of the least congested US consular posts in the world right now. (One secondary aggregator quoted ~49 days; the State-Department-derived figures say 15/8. Even the pessimistic number is fine against a March 2027 trip.)

With roughly **five months of runway**, a March 2027 trip is comfortably feasible.

### Cost per person

| Item | USD |
|---|---|
| MRV application fee (B1/B2) | 185 |
| Visa Integrity and Border Security Fee | 250 |
| **Max total per person** | **435** |
| **Group of 5** | **~2,175** |

Notes on the $250 fee: created by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed 4 July 2025. It is charged **only on issuance** — denied applicants don't pay it. Implementation was still rolling out post-by-post through mid-2026 and the Bureau of Consular Affairs had not issued full guidance as of March 2026, so Buenos Aires may or may not be collecting it yet. **Budget for it anyway.** It is indexed to inflation.

### Multiple entry — don't miss this

A B1/B2 for Argentines is normally issued as a **10-year multiple-entry** visa. This matters: any Bahamas or Aruba leg means **leaving and re-entering the United States**. Confirm "M" (multiple) on the visa foil before travelling. A single-entry visa would strand the whole beach plan.

### Action list

1. All five book B1/B2 interviews **now** (September/October 2026). The binding constraint is not the wait time — it's appointment slots evaporating ahead of a high-demand travel month.
2. Check each passport's expiry (needs validity beyond the trip).
3. Anyone with a prior US visa should check dropbox/interview-waiver eligibility — 8 days instead of 15.

### Overnight northbound — confirmed, with an asterisk

**Confirmed for AA 900, AA 908 and AR 1302** — all depart Buenos Aires at night and land in Miami at dawn. A Friday-night departure therefore lands Saturday morning and gives you the full Saturday. This is the classic pattern and it's what makes the "depart Fri 26 Feb night" framing work.

**But it is not universal.** AR 1304 (13:40) and LA 542 (10:55) are daytime departures arriving the same evening. Useful if you'd rather sleep in Buenos Aires and fly rested; wasteful if you want to save a hotel night.

Southbound returns are overwhelmingly overnight — every MIA→EZE service above departs in the evening and arrives the following morning.

### Other notes

- **LA 542 is a new route.** LATAM launched nonstop MIA–EZE on **1 December 2025**, so it will be ~15 months old at travel. It's the SCL–EZE–MIA tag. Published times vary a little between sources (10:55 and 13:10 both appear) — seasonal schedule variation, verify at booking.
- **AR 1302 does not fly Fridays.** Relevant for W3, where 12 March 2027 is a Friday.
- Combined route capacity is roughly 17–36 weekly departures depending on the source and season — three carriers competing keeps this route reasonably priced for a 4,421-mile sector.

## 3. One-stop EZE → MIA

| Airline | Via | Total travel time | W1 | W2 | W3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Avianca** | BOG | **~11h53** (shortest) | $760 | $950 | $880 |
| Copa | PTY | ~12–13h | $800 | $990 | $920 |
| Gol | GRU | ~13–14h | $780 | $960 | $900 |
| LATAM | SCL / LIM | ~13–14h | $840 | $1,050 | $970 |

**Avianca via Bogotá is the value pick.** BOG is the most-used connecting hub on this city pair (~24% of one-stop bookings), has the **shortest average total travel time** of any one-stop routing at 11h53, typical layover ~1h45, and Avianca repeatedly shows as the single cheapest carrier on the route (KAYAK lists it at $552 RT off-peak). You give up roughly 2.5–3 hours versus nonstop and save $150–250 per person. Across five people that's $750–1,250.

**Copa via Panama** is the operationally safest connection — Tocumen runs a high-frequency bank with 4–5 daily options to Miami, so a delay on leg 1 is recoverable. Segment times: EZE–PTY 7h27, PTY–MIA 3h08, layover 1h30–3h.

**Gol via São Paulo** is often the cheapest headline number, but GRU is a slow, sprawling transfer and Gol's long-haul product is thin. Verify Gol is actually marketing GRU–MIA itself on your dates rather than selling an American codeshare.

**LATAM via SCL/LIM** backtracks west or north and rarely beats its own nonstop by enough to justify the extra 4–5 hours.

**Is one-stop worth it for this group?** In W1, the gap is $900 direct vs $760 one-stop — $140 per person, $700 for the group, for ~3 extra hours each way. Marginal; I'd take the nonstop. In W2 the gap widens to ~$200 pp because connecting inventory is deeper in peak weeks, which makes it more compelling — but the better answer in W2 is to move the dates.

## 4. AEP — say it plainly

**Aeroparque is useless for this trip.**

AEP handles domestic Argentine routes plus short-haul regional South America only — Uruguay, Brazil, Paraguay, Chile, Bolivia, Peru, Colombia. Roughly 58 nonstop destinations across 8 countries on 9 airlines, **none of them in the United States**. There is no nonstop and no sensibly marketed one-stop product from AEP to Miami.

All long-haul North America traffic from Buenos Aires departs **EZE**, Terminal P.

The only scenario in which AEP appears is someone self-connecting a regional flight, which requires an inter-airport transfer across Buenos Aires (AEP→EZE is ~45–70 min by road, traffic-dependent). **Do not do this on separate tickets** — no protection if leg 1 slips.

**Book EZE.**

### And while we're ruling airports out: FLL

There are **no nonstop flights between EZE and FLL** in either direction. Shortest FLL–EZE itinerary is ~12h46 with a stop (Copa via PTY, Avianca, Delta, United all sell it). Fort Lauderdale is not a gateway option for the long leg. It's only relevant as a 45-minute / 30-mile drive north from Miami if a beach-leg flight happens to be cheaper out of FLL.

## 5. Onward beach leg from Miami

### ⚠️ Three corrections to the brief before the numbers

1. **Silver Airways no longer exists.** It abruptly ceased all operations and filed bankruptcy **effective 11 June 2025**, after a prospective buyer in its Chapter 11 declined to continue flying. It had served Bimini, Nassau and Freeport from Florida. Remove it from all planning — the surviving carriers on these routes are **American Airlines (incl. American Eagle)** and **Bahamasair**.
2. **GHB is Governor's Harbour, not North Eleuthera.** The brief listed "Eleuthera/Harbour Island GHB". Governor's Harbour (GHB) is in **central** Eleuthera. The airport for **Harbour Island** is **ELH (North Eleuthera)**. Both are served nonstop from MIA by American, but on very different frequencies. Details below.
3. **JetBlue does not fly MIA–AUA nonstop.** American is the only nonstop carrier from Miami to Aruba. JetBlue serves Aruba from FLL and JFK.

### The options

| Route | Airline(s) | Duration | Frequency | RT est. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MIA–NAS** Nassau | American + Bahamasair | 1h05–1h20 | Very high — Bahamasair 3×daily, AA multiple daily, ~45 flights/wk total | **$380** |
| **MIA–GGT** Great Exuma | American Eagle | ~1h40 | Up to 3×daily, ~26 nonstops/month | **$400** |
| **MIA–ELH** N. Eleuthera | American | 1h27 | Up to 3×daily | **$380** |
| **MIA–GHB** Gov's Harbour | American Eagle | ~1h18 | **4×weekly only** — Mon/Wed/Fri/Sun | **$400** |
| **MIA–AUA** Aruba | American (only nonstop) | 3h09 | ~13 AA/wk, ~21 total/wk, daily | **$520** |

**Fare basis:** Off-peak published figures are MIA–NAS from **$267** RT on Bahamasair (one-ways $126+), MIA–GGT from **$325** RT (one-ways from $138, AA one-ways ~$140, AA RT average ~$290), MIA–ELH around **$202–207**, GHB best AA RT around **$316**, and MIA–AUA around **$445–467** RT. I've marked all of these up for a March spring-break booking. Booking 8+ weeks out saves ~7% on the Nassau hop.

### Which to pick

**Nassau (NAS)** — easiest by a wide margin. Highest frequency, shortest hop, most hotel supply, and **US pre-clearance** means you clear US immigration *in Nassau* before flying home. The catch: Nassau is the single most spring-break-saturated destination in the Bahamas. You'd be flying into exactly the crowd you might be trying to escape.

**Great Exuma (GGT)** — far quieter and prettier. Swimming pigs, sandbars, the good stuff. But hotel inventory is thin, so March availability for five people needs locking down well before the flights.

**Harbour Island via ELH** — the premium pick, pink sand beaches. From ELH it's a ~5–10 min taxi to Three Island Dock, then a ~10 min water taxi to Dunmore Town. Budget **$15–20 pp each way, cash**, for taxi + ferry. Prices on the island match the reputation.

**Governor's Harbour (GHB)** — only if you specifically want central/south Eleuthera. The **4×weekly schedule (Mon/Wed/Fri/Sun)** constrains your beach dates hard. Route launched February 2024. Outbound MIA 11:40 → 12:58; return 13:24 → 14:50.

**Aruba (AUA)** — most expensive, longest, and it eats most of a travel day in each direction at 3h09. Upsides: outside the hurricane belt, far less spring-break-infested than Nassau, and **full US pre-clearance** so you land in Miami as a domestic arrival. Earliest departure MIA 10:05 → 13:05.

### The no-extra-flight alternative: Key West by road

| | |
|---|---|
| Distance | 166 miles / 267 km via US-1, the Overseas Highway |
| Drive time | **3h30–4h00 each way** |
| Traffic | Add 30–60 min each way Friday PM and Sunday PM |
| Route | 42 bridges; Key Largo → Islamorada → Marathon → Seven Mile Bridge |
| Cost, 5 sharing one minivan/SUV, 3 days | **~$350–500 total → ~$95 per person** |

This is the **big money saver**. It replaces a $380–520 per-person flight with a ~$95 per-person share — roughly **$1,400–2,100 saved across the group**. Reference points: solo car + gas + parking runs $130–180; two sharing runs $105–140 each, so five sharing a larger vehicle lands near $95 each.

Watch out for Key West parking — expensive and scarce. Most people park once and walk or cycle.

**There is no ferry from Miami to Key West.** The Key West Express sails only from **Fort Myers Beach** (year-round) and **Marco Island** (winter season, roughly late December–April) — both on Florida's *Gulf* coast, 3–4 hours' drive the wrong way from Miami. The crossing itself is ~3h30 at 40 mph, boarding 7am / departing 8am. Using it from Miami makes no sense. Drive US-1.

## 7. Seasonality — flag this hard

**The date choice is worth several hundred dollars per person.**

Early-to-mid March 2027 is peak US spring break in both Miami and the Bahamas. The entire month of March is described as extremely popular for Bahamas and Caribbean travel, because US school and college breaks stagger across all four weeks.

**The decisive data point: University of Miami's spring break 2027 runs 6–13 March.** That is a day-for-day exact overlap with W2.

**Ranking: W1 best → W3 middle → W2 worst.**

W1 should come in roughly **$200–250 per person cheaper on airfare** than W2, with a larger saving again on Nassau and Harbour Island hotel rates, where spring-break inventory is the real squeeze.

**Countervailing factor from the Argentine side:** January is the most expensive month on Buenos Aires–Miami (~$1,055 RT) because of Argentine summer holidays; April is cheapest (~$728). February/March sits below the January peak on outbound Argentine demand while being pushed *up* by US inbound spring-break demand. The two effects partly cancel — which is why my estimates land above the annual typical band of $697–1,008 but below January levels. KAYAK notes the spread between cheapest and dearest month averages 44% on this route.

**Booking advice:**

- **Lodging is the harder constraint than airfare.** Bahamas spring-break resorts are commonly **full before 1 December**. Lock accommodation before you lock flights.
- Book the Miami–Nassau hop **8+ weeks out** for ~7% savings.
- For 5 people in peak season, **book as one reservation** or you'll be split across fare buckets and pay the highest one.
- Consider shifting W1 earlier if anyone is flexible — late February drops away from the peak fast.

## 9. Confidence and caveats

**High confidence (verified against multiple sources):**
- Argentina is not in the VWP; B1/B2 required
- Buenos Aires wait times ~15 days interview / ~8 days dropbox
- Flight numbers AA 900, AA 908, AR 1302, AR 1304, LA 542 and their routings
- AEP has no US service; no EZE–FLL nonstop
- Silver Airways defunct since 11 June 2025
- UMiami spring break 2027 = 6–13 March
- No Miami–Key West ferry
- Bahamas and Aruba visa-free for Argentines

**Medium confidence:**
- Exact departure/arrival times — seasonal schedule variation, and LA 542 shows two different published departure times across sources. Verify at booking.
- The $250 visa integrity fee being collected in Buenos Aires specifically — implementation was still rolling out post-by-post as of mid-2026.

**Estimates, flagged as such (`is_estimate: true` throughout the JSON):**
- **All 2027 fares.** February/March 2027 is ~5 months out and airlines have not opened or priced those date ranges in a way that's quotable today. Every price here is anchored on September 2026 published fare ranges for the same route and season, with a documented spring-break premium layered on. Treat them as planning numbers with roughly ±20% spread, not quotes.
- Car rental total for the Keys — depends heavily on vehicle class and how far ahead you book.

**Nothing in this file is an invented flight number or a nonexistent route.** Where the brief named something that doesn't exist (Silver Airways, JetBlue MIA–AUA nonstop, a Miami–Key West ferry) or was mislabelled (GHB for Harbour Island), it's called out as a correction rather than quietly filled in.
