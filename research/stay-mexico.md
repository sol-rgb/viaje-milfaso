# Mexico — 9 days, 5 people, early March 2027

Working notes. Research done September 2026. Companion file: `stay-mexico.json`.

Group: 5 friends, late 20s/30s, design-conscious, good food, a scene but not a party hostel.
Flying from Buenos Aires. 2 nights CDMX + 5 nights Pacific coast.

---

## 0. The single most important number in this file

The budget rule is **USD 100 per person per night**. The brief assumed "2 sharing a room, so a double up to ~USD 200 is in budget."

**That maths breaks at five people.** Five people need three rooms, not two and a half. So:

```
pp cost = (3 × room rate) / 5 = room rate × 0.6
budget ceiling on a double room = 100 / 0.6 = USD 166
```

Not USD 200. **USD 166.** That single correction disqualifies most of the interesting hotels in both stops, and it is the reason the recommendation below tilts hard toward whole houses.

A whole house sleeping 5 divides by 5, not by 0.6:

```
whole-house ceiling = USD 500 / night
```

At USD 500/night in La Punta you can rent a brutalist villa with an infinity pool. At USD 166/night you cannot get a room in one decent design hotel on the coast. This is the whole argument.

Window 2 (6–13 Mar) sits directly on it; window 1 catches the tail.

### Why it beats the alternatives

|---|---|---|

| Huatulco | Similar flight | A resort-and-bays town. Planned, tidy, no design scene, nothing to walk to at night. Wrong crowd — families and all-inclusives. |
| Zihuatanejo / Troncones | MEX–ZIH + 40 min transfer | Genuinely beautiful and very quiet. ### The honest bit about Puerto Escondido's prices

It is not the cheap secret it was five years ago:

- United launched direct Houston–PXM service in **April 2025**, which changed the buyer pool.
- Boutique rooms near **Carrizalillo and La Punta now run USD 150–300/night in high season — comparable to mid-range Tulum**.
- Premium La Punta property gained **20–25% in 2025** alone; Zicatela appreciated ~18–22%/yr 2023–25.
- The famous architectural hotels are all out of reach at this budget: Casona Sforza ~USD 392, Terrestre ~USD 340+, Hotel Humano ~USD 286, Casa TO ~USD 249, Hotel Escondido (also 45 min inland).

**But** the villa market has not inflated at the same rate. 3–4 bedroom houses with private pools in La Punta / Brisas de Zicatela sit at **USD 375–420/night whole = USD 75–84 each**. Better architecture, more space, lower per-head price than any hotel here. That is the play.

### Getting the three beaches right

This distinction gets muddled in most guides. It matters.

**LA PUNTA** (officially Punta Zicatela / Brisas de Zicatela, administratively Santa María Colotepec, ~15 min from PXM)
The south end of the long Zicatela beach, where the sandbar turns and throws a **soft, long left-hand point break**. Dirt streets, palapa roofs, yoga, the best small restaurants and coffee, and the surf-and-design crowd. This is the group's neighbourhood and where you learn to surf. Sunset ritual on the rocks at the point every evening.

**ZICATELA**
The 3 km strip north of La Punta. Home of the **Mexican Pipeline** — a heavy, fast, shore-breaking barrel that is genuinely dangerous and **absolutely not a swimming beach**. It is the big-wave strip and the nightlife strip: surf shops, bars, loud until late. Go there to watch the pros and to go out. Do not sleep there unless you want bass through the wall.

**CARRIZALILLO**
Neither of the above. A small horseshoe cove on the west side of town, reached by **170 concrete steps** cut into the cliff. The **only reliably swimmable beach in Puerto Escondido** — soft whitewater, gentle shelf, no Zicatela rip. Beginner surf. Palapa bars serving micheladas and grilled fish on the sand. Best sunset in town. Playa Manzanillo next door is calmer still.

**Plan: sleep and surf at La Punta, swim at Carrizalillo, go out at Zicatela.**

### The quiet villages (1–1.5 h east on Highway 200)

- **San Agustinillo** — tiny, one crescent of sand, the best swim of the three, the least developed.
- **Mazunte** — Centro Mexicano de la Tortuga (the national sea turtle research centre, all seven Mexican nesting species), the natural-cosmetics cooperative, and Punta Cometa. Busier every year, strong wellness/hippie overlay.
- **Zipolite** — Mexico's only legal nude beach. Late, slightly wilder scene. **Vicious rip current** — do not swim there after drinking.

Worth one long day trip. **Not** worth splitting a 5-night stay.

## 3. CDMX — which neighbourhood

**ROMA NORTE is the right base.** Highest density inside a 15-minute walk of what this group wants: Contramar, Rosetta, Expendio de Maíz, Masala y Maíz (technically Juárez), Licorería Limantour, Mercado de Medellín, the Álvaro Obregón galleries, and a stock of Porfirian townhouses and Art Deco blocks that explains why half the good Airbnbs in the city are here.

**CONDESA** — one park over, equally good. Quieter, greener, more residential. Parque México and Avenida Amsterdam (a 1920s racetrack turned into an oval street). Slightly better value on whole apartments.

**JUÁREZ** — the value play. Same 1900s architecture, harder-edged cocktail-bar scene, hotels like Casa Prim at roughly half the Roma rate, 10 minutes' walk into Roma Norte.

**POLANCO — wrong call for this group.** It is money, not taste: luxury retail, glass towers, Pujol and Quintonil, and nothing to walk to at night that isn't a reservation. Book Polanco for the restaurants, sleep in Roma or Condesa.

### Capacity trap in CDMX

Several of the best design hotels here are **tiny** and physically cannot take five people comfortably:

- **Octavia Casa** — 6 rooms, all kings for 2. Five people = half the hotel.
- **Ignacia Guest House** — 9 suites.
- **Círculo Mexicano** — 25 rooms, fine on capacity but USD 159 pp.

The Airbnb route in Roma/Condesa is both cheaper and materially easier at this group size.

## 7. Activities — the specifics

### CDMX

**Casa Estudio Luis Barragán** — 1948 house-studio, UNESCO, Tacubaya. Guided visit only, small groups. **Book the moment you have dates.** The site states in its own terms that entry is not guaranteed without an advance ticket, tickets are "strictly subject to availability," and slots for five on one tour go weeks out. Min age 12, no exceptions. Email replies take 2–4 business days, so don't leave it to a phone call. → `visit.casaluisbarragan.org/mx`

**Museo Frida Kahlo (Casa Azul)** — Londres 247, Coyoacán. **Booking is the hard part**: timed entry, sells out days-to-weeks ahead, sold only via `boletos.museofridakahlo.org.mx` or a kiosk at the museum shop. Closed Mondays. Tue 10–18, Wed 11–18, Thu–Sun 10–18. **"Noche Azul" Thursdays 17:15–21:00 is the least crowded slot.**

**Museo Nacional de Antropología** — **MXN 210** general admission (verified, official INAH site). Tue–Sun 09:00–18:00, **closed Mondays**. Sunday free entry applies **only to Mexican nationals and residents**, so a foreign group pays either way — but a Sunday visit means Chapultepec is at its best (families, balloon sellers, food carts) while the museum is at its most crowded. Pedro Ramírez Vázquez's 1964 building, the cantilevered umbrella fountain, the Sun Stone, the Olmec heads.

**Teotihuacán** — sunrise balloon lifts off ~06:00–06:30, lands for breakfast, then you walk the site ahead of the coaches. Price **estimated USD 130–180 pp** (operator sites were not fetchable — verify). Site admission ~MXN 100 separate. **You can no longer climb the pyramids.** Ground-only alternative: leave CDMX 06:00, be at the gate for the 08:00 opening, have the Avenue of the Dead nearly to yourselves. Either way it eats the entire day — swap it against Coyoacán, don't try both.

**Xochimilco** — boats are priced **per boat per hour (~MXN 600), not per person**, so five splitting one trajinera is the cheapest thing on the list (~USD 20 pp for 3 hours). Embarcadero Nuevo Nativitas, or quieter Cuemanco. Best Sat/Sun when it's full of Mexican families rather than empty. Agree price and hours before stepping on. Bring your own mezcal and limes.

**Lucha libre, Arena México** — CMLL, Colonia Doctores, 16,500 seats. **Friday 20:30 is the big card**; also Tue 19:30 and Sun 17:00. Buy decent seats, you want to see the masks. Doctores is rough — **Ubers door to door, don't walk from the metro at night.**

**Mercado de Medellín** — Campeche 101, Roma Sur, 10 min south of Roma Norte. Colombian/Cuban/Venezuelan stalls, arepas, Oaxacan cheese, mole by the kilo, cheap ceviche, fondas doing comida corrida. Not a tourist market. Done by 14:00.

**Roma/Condesa crawl** — Plaza Río de Janeiro → Colima and Álvaro Obregón for the mansions and galleries → Panadería Rosetta (Colima 179) → **Expendio de Maíz Sin Nombre** (Yucatán 84: no menu, no prices, they bring plates until you stop them — go at 13:00, the queue is brutal after 14:00) → across Veracruz into Condesa for the Art Deco around Parque México and Avenida Amsterdam.

**Mezcalería night** — La Clandestina (Álvaro Obregón 298) → Licorería Limantour (Álvaro Obregón 106, World's 50 Best Bars) → **Bósforo** (Luis Moya 31, Centro: black room, no sign, no menu, best mezcal list in the city; opens ~20:00, full by 22:00). Uber between them.

## 9. Getting around

**CDMX.** Uber is the default — USD 4–8 for most Roma↔Polanco trips, safe, and the only sane way home after mezcal. **Do not hail street taxis.** Didi is often cheaper. Metro is MXN 5 and genuinely fast (Lines 1 and 3 are the useful ones) but packed and pickpocketed at rush hour, no lifts — fine for two with a day pack, awkward for five. Metrobús Line 7 runs up Reforma and drops at Antropología. **Roma Norte, Roma Sur and Condesa are one continuous walkable grid** — no car needed between them. Ecobici is good on Amsterdam and through Chapultepec. Allow 60–90 min to the airport at rush hour; **MEX T1 and T2 are far apart**, connected by an inter-terminal train — know your terminal.

**MEX → PXM.** Direct, ~1h20, Volaris and Aeroméxico Connect. Returns seen USD 40–70 booked ahead. **Take the morning flight** — PXM's runway sits on the coast and afternoon crosswinds cause diversions to Huatulco, which means a 3-hour bus back.

**Puerto Escondido.** **Colectivos** are the local system: shared vans/pickups on fixed routes along the main road between La Punta, Zicatela, the Adoquín and Rinconada. **MXN 10–15 a person**, flag them anywhere, no schedule, stop running ~21:00–22:00. **Taxis**: MXN 60–80 La Punta↔town, MXN 120–150 to Carrizalillo. **No meters — agree the fare before getting in.** Uber coverage is patchy and locally contested; don't rely on it. Mototaxis do short La Punta hops for MXN 30. A rental car or two scooters is worth it if you want Mazunte and the lagoons independently.

**The Oaxaca coast road.** **Highway 200** runs east: ~1h15 to San Agustinillo/Mazunte/Zipolite, ~2h30 to Huatulco. Paved and scenic but two-lane, with topes in every village, slow trucks, and stretches under repair after hurricane seasons. **Do not drive it at night** — no lighting, livestock on the road, occasional checkpoints. The inland road to Oaxaca City (Hwy 175 through the sierra) used to be 6–7 hours of nausea-inducing switchbacks; the new **Barranca Larga–Ventanilla highway has cut it to ~2h30–3h**, so adding Oaxaca City is now actually feasible — just not on a 9-day trip with 5 beach nights.

## 10. Weather reality, March

**CDMX.** Tail of the dry season, about as good as it gets: highs 25–27°C, lows 10–12°C, almost no rain, very strong UV at 2,240 m. Two real caveats:
1. **It's cold at night and in the shade.** Bring a jacket — and note several heritage hotels (Casa Decu among them) have no heating.
2. **March is the worst month of the year for air quality.** Thermal inversions trap pollution; CDMX issues ozone contingencies most often in March and April. Check the index if anyone's asthmatic.
Altitude also hits harder than people expect on day one — go easy on the mezcal the first night.

**Puerto Escondido.** Peak dry season and close to perfect. Highs 31–33°C, lows 21–23°C, sea 27–28°C, moderate humidity, statistically almost zero rain — **March is the driest month here**. The sun between 11:00 and 16:00 is brutal and the UV index runs extreme; the shade-and-siesta rhythm is necessity, not laziness.

**Surf:** March sits *between* the big southern-hemisphere swell seasons. Zicatela's Mexican Pipeline is working but not at its April–October monster size, and **La Punta's left is at its most forgiving — the best month of the year to learn.** Carrizalillo is calm and swimmable throughout. Still the tail of humpback season, so dawn boats see them.

**Mosquitoes** are at their annual low in the dry season — but the lagoons at dusk are another matter. Take repellent for Manialtepec, and remember you can't wear it if you're swimming in the bioluminescence.

## 12. Bottom line

1. **Travel 27 Feb – 7 Mar or 6–13 Mar 2027.** Both are clear of Semana Santa (21–28 March). Avoid 13–21 March — it ends on Palm Sunday.
2. **Sleep in Roma Norte or Condesa in CDMX**, not Polanco.
3. **Go to Puerto Escondido. Base in La Punta.** Swim at Carrizalillo, go out at Zicatela, one long day to Mazunte and Punta Cometa.
